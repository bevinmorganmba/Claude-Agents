# Five-year plan

Bevin's own words, recorded August 2026. Danbury reasons against this file and
nothing else — if an answer can't be traced to something here, she should say
the plan is silent rather than fill the gap with generic advice.

## The horizon (2031)

**Out of Tardus.** No longer working there. Financial coaching only for family,
close friends, or clients paying $1,000 per session.

**Invest in S. DeKalb is fully funded** and paying a full-time salary. Actively
and consistently bringing development to South DeKalb. Networking with
investors, business owners, and government officials to get things done in the
county.

**The Salon runs without hands-on intervention.** Responsible for charting the
course, not for steering the ship. *Odysseus — hands get dirty only when they
need to.*

**Five CFO clients** at different stages of growth, all on a clean once-monthly
meeting cadence.

**Options account in the mid six figures**, averaging 1% return per week.

**Living in a 4-plex with commercial space that pays him to live there.**

**Every last month of the quarter taken off completely** to recharge.

Possibly running or exiting a small business created to demonstrate
entrepreneurial credibility. *(See the open tension at the foot of this file.)*

## The three bets

### 1. Options trading — covered puts, Low Stress Options framework
Paid off when: account is **mid six figures or better**, averaging **1% return
per week**.

### 2. Invest in S. DeKalb
Paid off when: it pays a **full-time salary of $120K+** and **at least one
development project** has been ushered into the county.

### 3. BFF — the fractional CFO business
Paid off when: earning **$15K/month from 5 clients** on a monthly meeting
cadence, with plenty of free time to learn, grow, and recharge.

An opportunity that advances none of these three is, by definition, off-plan.
Say so plainly rather than looking for a way it might connect.

## Deliberately not doing

Fast no, not a thoughtful maybe:

- **Personal financial coaching.** (The 2031 exception is family, close
  friends, or $1,000/session — not a general reopening.)
- **Excessive networking.** No conferences where he is not a speaker.
- **Spinning up new businesses**, or pursuing anything for exit or IPO
  potential.
- **Working more than 20 hours per week.** This is a plan constraint, not an
  aspiration — treat proposals that need more than that as failing on cost.

## Constraints that are real

- **Money.** The binding constraint today.
- **Tardus obligations.** Consuming hours that the three bets need.
- **Time is not the constraint he believes it is** — his own read is that
  there is enough time to build the systems that make all of this doable. Take
  him at his word; don't argue him into "you're overcommitted" unless the
  arithmetic in front of you actually says so.

## The number that governs everything

**$7,500 gross per month from non-Tardus sources.** That is the threshold at
which leaving Tardus becomes viable — enough to live on and keep investing.

This is the single most decision-relevant figure in the plan. Anything that
moves recurring non-Tardus revenue toward $7,500/month is doing the real work;
anything that doesn't is, at best, a later-phase concern. When placing an
opportunity, say what it does to this number.

## Open tension — flag, don't resolve

The horizon includes *"potentially running or exiting a small business I
created to show my chops as an entrepreneur."* The not-doing list rules out
*"spinning up new businesses or pursuing something with exit or IPO
potential."*

These point in opposite directions. Until Bevin settles it, treat the
**not-doing list as binding** and surface the contradiction when it becomes
load-bearing — don't quietly pick a side.
